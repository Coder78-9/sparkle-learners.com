Reading

Summarizing Texts and Making Inferences

Now that you know how to find the main idea and supporting details, you will learn to summarize texts in your own words. Summarizing means explaining the most important ideas without copying everything. You will also practice making inferences — using clues from the text to figure out information that isn’t directly stated. For example, if a paragraph says, “The ground was covered in snow and the it was falling snow” you can infer that it probably snowing and cold. These skills help you understand texts more deeply and answer more challenging questions.

Science

Moon Phases, Tides, and Gravity

Building on what you learned about moon phases and tides, the next step is understanding gravity’s role. The Moon’s gravity pulls on Earth’s oceans, causing high and low tides. The Sun also affects tides, making some tides higher (spring tides) and others lower (neap tides). You will also explore how gravity keeps the Moon in orbit around Earth and Earth in orbit around the Sun. Understanding these patterns helps explain how celestial bodies interact and why tides, eclipses, and the Moon’s phases happen regularly.
